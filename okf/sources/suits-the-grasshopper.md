**Claim this bundle leans on.** A game has a *prelusory goal* (a state of affairs,
describable without the game), *constitutive rules* (which forbid the most efficient
means to it), and the **lusory attitude**: accepting the rules *because* they make the
activity possible. Short form: *"the voluntary attempt to overcome unnecessary
obstacles."*

**What it settles here.** Suits answers Wittgenstein by locating the definition in the
*player's stance* rather than in the artifact. That is why the author's "players" belong
in the vocabulary at all: a world with rules and no one who has agreed to them is a
simulation, not a game. [What a game is](/concepts/what-a-game-is.md) §2 keeps *player*
as a noun distinct from *avatar* for this reason, and it is why the OS reading in §7 holds:
a user who accepts the system's rules to get what the system makes possible has the lusory
attitude toward it.
