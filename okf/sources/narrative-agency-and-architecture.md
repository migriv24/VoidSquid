**Murray (1997).** Three pleasures particular to digital narrative: **agency** (the
satisfying power to take meaningful action and see its results), **immersion**, and
**transformation**. Agency is Murray's name for what
[Salen & Zimmerman](/sources/salen-zimmerman-rules-of-play.md) later formalise as
meaningful play.

**Jenkins (2004).** Game designers are *"narrative architects"*: they build spaces in
which stories can happen. Four modes: **evocative** spaces (that recall stories the
player already knows), **enacted** stories (the player performs the arc), **embedded**
narrative (information placed in the world to be found: a letter, a wrecked room), and
**emergent** narrative (what happens when the systems run, as in The Sims).

**Aarseth (1997).** **Ergodic** literature requires *nontrivial effort* to traverse. A
game's narrative is not read; it is traversed, and the path is part of the text.

**What it settles here.** The author wants *narratives*, *NPCs that feel alive*, and
worlds where *assets are not static*. Jenkins gives the structure: embedded narrative is
**content on runes in places** (so it is already an `.ink` with text attached); enacted
narrative is a **graph of beats with conditions** (authored structure, the forge's);
emergent narrative is **the runtime's dynamics** and cannot be authored, only invited.
[What a game is](/concepts/what-a-game-is.md) §5 uses these three as the three ways a
Void Squid game holds its story. The AI side (who generates the story at play time) is
[AI directors and drama managers](/sources/ai-directors-and-drama-managers.md).
