**Claim this bundle leans on.** Games are puzzles the brain enjoys solving, and the fun
is the moment of grasping a pattern. When the pattern is fully learned the game becomes
boring; the designer's job is to keep the curriculum ahead of the player.

**What it settles here.** The author's fourth belief, that a game offers *"avenues of
upgrades"* and that these *"carry themes and messages of the world itself,"* is Koster's
teacher read with [Bogost](/sources/bogost-procedural-rhetoric.md): the upgrade graph is
the syllabus, and what it teaches is the game's claim about what *better* means.
[What a game is](/concepts/what-a-game-is.md) §4 therefore treats the upgrade graph as
authored structure the forge can query, not as a runtime detail.
