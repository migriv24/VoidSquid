**Claim this bundle leans on.** Games are **half-real**: the rules are real (you really
win or lose) and the world is fictional (the dragon is not). The **classic game model**
has six features: rules; variable, quantifiable outcomes; valorisation of outcomes
(some are better); player effort; player attachment to the outcome; negotiable
consequences. Juul uses Tetris and abstract games as the limiting case where the fiction
half is nearly empty.

**What it settles here.** The split *rules / fiction* is the split the family already
has: rules are **data the runtime reads** (compiled from the forge), the fiction is **the
mantle** (runes, tags, links, content). Squid's games require both halves; the vocabulary
must describe games with one. *Valorisation of outcomes* is the formal name for the
author's "the game saying *this is what it means to be BETTER*," and it ties to
[Bogost](/sources/bogost-procedural-rhetoric.md). See
[what a game is](/concepts/what-a-game-is.md) §4 and §6.
